Our Heritage

A Legacy Written in Stone

Built in 1924 by the merchant prince Krishnaswami Saratha, this palatial estate stands as one of the finest examples of Indo-Saracenic architecture in the region. Its high vaulted corridors, hand-painted frescoes, and teak-wood antechambers whisper stories of a time when grandeur was a way of life.

Spanning over 18,000 sq ft across four wings, Saratha Vilas has hosted royalty, diplomats, and artists. Today, it is preserved as a living museum — every room a chapter, every corridor a verse.

The Vilas Experience

Curated for the discerning visitor
01

Architecture & Design

Explore the estate's Indo-Saracenic façade, its Moorish arches, intricate jali screens, and ornate stucco work — a vocabulary of design that blurs East and West.

02

Curated Collections

Over 600 artefacts — bronze Nataraja sculptures, colonial-era silverware, hand-knotted Kochi carpets, and correspondence from the Dewan's court — are preserved within these walls.

03

Garden & Grounds

Four acres of manicured gardens surround the vilas — century-old rain trees, a rose terrace, a lily pond, and a secluded pavilion once used for evening recitals.

04

Living History

Seasonal events, classical music evenings, and storytelling walks bring the heritage alive. Saratha Vilas is not a relic — it is a living, breathing chapter of South India's cultural story.

05

Heritage Stays

Select suites within the estate have been thoughtfully restored to offer overnight stays. Fall asleep beneath hand-painted ceilings, wake to the fragrance of jasmine in the courtyard.

06

Digital Archive

Decades of photographs, letters, and blueprints have been digitised into an accessible public archive — a gift to historians, researchers, and the simply curious.
